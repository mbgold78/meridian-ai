# Task 2 — Read a cascade's timeline, including a decline

What this task teaches: how a critique travels the chain of command, and how
to read a formal decline as the documented governance outcome it is.

A **cascade** is a critique an executive issues down the chain. Each level
that holds it either takes the work, refines it and passes it down, or
formally declines with reasoning that travels back **up**.

## Read a cascade

1. From the dashboard's **Cascades you've issued** grid, open the card with
   the red **Decline pending** chip (**Inspect cascade**).

2. Read the detail top to bottom:

{{capture:cascade-detail-decline-pending}}

   - **Header** — the aggregate state, the original critique *verbatim* (it
     travels the chain unchanged), who issued it and when, and the
     **Acceptance criteria** panel: what "fixed" means, in the issuer's own
     words.
   - **Level-by-level transparency** — every level with its owner, date,
     and state pill: **Looking** (amber — holds it, hasn't decided),
     **Cascaded** (blue — refined and passed down, refined wording shown),
     **Absorbed** (green — took the work), **Declined** (red — formally
     pushed back, *its reasoning is shown right there*).
   - **Action timeline** — the append-only log of every recorded step,
     oldest first and numbered, each with actor, level, date, and attached
     reasoning. This is the issuer's single audit trail; it is only present
     because this page is live engine data.
   - **Cascade summary** — the resting state in one line.

3. Read the declined level's reasoning, then re-read the acceptance
   criteria. That pairing — *what was asked* against *why a level said no* —
   is the decision now waiting on the issuer.

**What you cannot do here yet:** respond or decline from this page. Those
verbs are recorded by your engagement team today —
[not-yet/cascade-respond.md](not-yet/cascade-respond.md) says exactly what
exists and what is coming.

## You'll know you've read it right when…

…you can retell the cascade as a story — *who asked for what, how each level
answered, and why the declining level pushed back* — entirely from the level
pills and the numbered timeline, and the decline reads to you as a recorded
position, not a failure.
